## §0 Start here: the one-paragraph model

iLaunchify keeps its existing two fees exactly as they are (creator tier fee 15/12/8, manufacturer merit withhold 4.5/2.5/0) and adds **one pass-through line: US sales tax**, computed per ship-to address by Stripe Tax, collected on the creator's charge, remitted by the platform as a **marketplace facilitator**. Tax is never revenue, never in the fee base, never folded all-in. Most orders should carry **no tax at all** once the creator has a **resale certificate** on file, because creators buy production for resale. There is **no separate payment-processing fee line**: the tier fee covers it.

Invariant to preserve throughout: **quote === stored === charged.** Tax is computed on OUR side and fed into `computeOrderPricing`; Stripe never adds a cent the pricer didn't price.

## §2 Decoding the Etsy screenshot (what each line actually is)

Buyer paid $86.83 = items $60.66 + shipping $23.52 - coupon $3.03 + tax $5.68.

1. **Transaction fee $5.28** = 6.5% x $81.15. Etsy's fee base = items + shipping (post-discount). Shipping is IN their base because Etsy sellers set and pocket shipping, and historically gamed $0.20 items + $20 shipping. That is Etsy's version of our LOCKED fee-base rule ("in the base iff the seller sets the price AND keeps the proceeds"). Same rule, different facts.
2. **Payment processing fee $2.85** = 3% x $86.83 + $0.25, charged on the GROSS **including the tax portion**. Etsy's transaction fee is low (6.5%), so processing is itemized separately.
3. **Tax paid by buyer $5.68**: not a fee. Etsy collected it from the buyer as a marketplace facilitator and remits it to the state. It appears as a "deduction" only because it passed through the seller's payment. Etsy earns $0 on it.

Seller nets $86.83 - $13.81 = $73.02.

## §3 The three questions, answered for iLaunchify

### Q1. Transaction fee? YES, and it already exists.

The creator tier fee (Maker 15 / Builder 12 / Agency 8, `resolveCreatorFeeBps`, Option C all-in presentation, "Administrative fee") IS our transaction fee. **No change.** Do NOT copy Etsy's shipping-inclusive base: our shipping is EasyPost-rated with our own margin, partners cannot shift price into it, so the LOCKED production-only fee base stands (re-derived, same answer as 2026-07-15).

### Q2. Payment processing fee? NO separate line.

- Etsy itemizes processing because its transaction fee is only 6.5%. Ours is 8-15% and covers ~3% processing with room to spare.
- A visible processing line contradicts Option C (all-in creator presentation) and reads as a junk fee.
- If margin ever needs it: fold a processing allowance into `FeeRule` (admin-tunable bps), never a new creator-facing line. That keeps "no hardcoded fee" and ONE-pricer intact.
- **Cost fact to carry:** Stripe, like Etsy, charges processing on the gross INCLUDING tax and shipping. The platform bears ~3% of the tax it merely passes through. Accept as cost of doing business (Etsy does); revisit only if tax-heavy volume makes it material.

### Q3. Tax paid by buyer? YES. This is the missing piece, and the only one that can hurt the platform if skipped.

- **Who owes the obligation: the PLATFORM.** iLaunchify lists third-party partners' production and processes the payment, which is the definition of a **marketplace facilitator**. Every sales-tax state (45 + DC) has a facilitator law: once the platform has nexus in a state, the platform (not the manufacturer, not the creator) must collect and remit on facilitated sales. Uncollected tax becomes platform liability plus penalties, and it is a standard diligence item in a raise.
- **"Per state?" is the wrong granularity.** US sales tax is destination-based on the ship-to address at state + county + city + special-district level, roughly 13,000 jurisdictions with shifting rates and product-category rules (e.g. food for human consumption is exempt or reduced in most states; supplements often are NOT; packaging materials have their own rules). Nobody hand-builds this. **Use Stripe Tax** (we are all-in on Stripe; it slots into the existing rails).
- **Where we must collect, initially: Georgia only.** Physical nexus (Pavel + operations in GA). Register with the GA Department of Revenue before charging real money. Every other state activates only when ECONOMIC nexus trips: ~$100k of sales into the state (many states have dropped the old 200-transaction prong; thresholds vary). Platform-wide facilitated sales aggregate toward each state's threshold. Stripe Tax threshold monitoring watches this and tells us when a state trips; we register, flip the state on, done.

## §4 The iLaunchify-specific lever Etsy doesn't have: resale exemption

Etsy's buyers are consumers; ours are **businesses buying production for resale**. Sales for resale are exempt in essentially every state when the buyer holds a valid **resale certificate**, and packaging that ships with the resold product generally qualifies under resale/packaging exemptions.

Design:

- Creator uploads a resale certificate **per Brand** (state-issued; multi-state via the MTC Uniform Sales & Use Tax Certificate where accepted).
- Admin (or later, automated) review marks the Brand exempt; we set the Stripe Tax customer to `reverse`/exempt status. Orders for that brand then price `taxCents: 0` legitimately.
- **No certificate on file = charge destination tax.** Never default to exempt.
- Certificates expire in some states: store `expiresAt`, notify before expiry (packages/notifications), revert to taxable on lapse.
- Snapshot the exemption basis on the Order (`taxExemptionSource`), consistent with how `platformFeeSource` snapshots work. Audit every status change (packages/audit).

This is compliance AND a marketing line: "Upload your resale certificate, pay no sales tax on production."

Two taxable surfaces remain even with a cert:
1. **HOLD_AT_MANUFACTURER / samples shipped to the creator themselves** may not qualify as resale in some states (the sample is "used", not resold). V1: charge tax on sample orders unless the state clearly exempts; revisit with counsel.
2. **Subscriptions (SaaS tiers)**: taxable in ~20 states regardless of resale certs. Stripe Tax on Billing handles it; webhook already stores `invoice.tax`.

## §5 Implementation (fits every locked invariant)

**Do NOT enable session `automatic_tax`.** That path lets Stripe add a number our pricer never saw. Instead:

- **T1. Stripe Tax registration + origin address** configured in the Stripe dashboard (GA first). `integration-registry.ts` gets a status row.
- **T2. `resolveTaxCents` in packages/payments** (or a thin `packages/tax`): wraps the **Stripe Tax Calculations API**. Inputs: ship-to address, line items (production, shipping) with product tax codes, customer exemption status. Output: `taxCents` + calculation id. DI'd http like the EasyPost gateway so tests are network-free. Returns 0 when the ship-to state has no active registration (pre-nexus sales are legally untaxed by us; the creator may owe use tax, not our problem to collect).
- **T3. Thread it through the ONE pricer**: checkout estimate and `placeOrder` both call T2 and pass `taxCents` into `computeOrderPricing`. The charge remains the pricer's `totalCents`. Quote===stored===charged preserved by construction. Snapshot `taxCalculationId`, `taxExemptionSource` on Order.
- **T4. Post-payment**: create the Stripe Tax **transaction** from the calculation id (webhook on payment success), so Stripe's remittance reports are accurate. Refunds (currently off) must reverse the tax transaction when they arrive.
- **T5. Resale certificate model + admin surface**: `ResaleCertificate` (uuid id per FREEZE H3): brandId, state(s), fileKey, status FSM (SUBMITTED → VERIFIED / REJECTED / EXPIRED), expiresAt, audit on every transition. Admin review queue (v2 admin surface pattern). Creator upload in /settings or Brand settings.
- **T6. Presentation**: tax is a **separate visible line labeled "Tax"** on creator itemized surfaces and checkout. Option C's all-in folding applies ONLY to the administrative fee; tax is exactly the "only real government tax may be a tax line" case the Option C brief reserved the word for. Exempt orders show no tax line (optionally "Tax: $0.00 (resale certificate on file)").
- **T7. Gates**: everything behind an admin LogisticsSetting-style gate + per-state registration list (admin → Finance). Shadow mode first: compute and LOG taxCents without charging, compare against expectations, then flip. Same shadow-inert pattern as MeritPolicy.
- **T8. Money map + pins**: extend `money-map.test.ts` and order-pricing pins: tax never in fee base, merit never on tax, sum(dispatch legs) === production unaffected by tax, transfer math unaffected (tax is never transferred to partners).

Explicitly NOT in scope: charging end buyers (they never touch iLaunchify; the creator's channel handles consumer tax: Etsy/TikTok as facilitators there, Shopify = creator's own obligation), VAT/international (Markets V1 is US-only), and any tax on the platform fee itself beyond what Stripe Tax's product codes say.

## §6 What "harming the platform" would look like (the anti-goals)

1. **Folding tax into all-in prices**: platform eats a cost it cannot control and violates the "never label/hide a fee as tax, never a tax as fee" rule. Tax must be a visible pass-through.
2. **Treating tax as revenue**: it is a liability from the moment collected. Keep it out of every margin/fee expression (the pricer already does).
3. **Hand-built state tables**: instant staleness + audit exposure, and it violates the "buy proven tools, conservative defaults" doctrine.
4. **Collecting where unregistered / not collecting where registered**: both are violations. Registration list is the SSOT and gates collection per state (T7).
5. **Defaulting brands to exempt** without a verified certificate.
6. **Session-side `automatic_tax`**: re-opens the quote-vs-charge divergence class the money path was cleaned to eliminate.
