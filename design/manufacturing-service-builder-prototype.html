Manufacturing service

Service basics

You make the formula. This builder captures your floor and your defaults, not your prices: a formula's price is product-specific, so it belongs on the product. Everything here flows into every product you build, so you set it once instead of retyping it into each one.

How this differs from the print and co-pack builders, on purpose. A printer sells a piece, so they price a curve. A co-packer sells operations, so they price a menu. You sell a formula, and no two formulas cost the same, so your prices live on your products. What lives HERE is what every product inherits: your batch physics, your hard filters, your commercial defaults, and your standing.

Step 2

Your batches

The batch is the unit of truth on a blending floor, the way a press is in a print shop and a line is on a co-pack floor. You cannot make half a batch. That single fact is where your MOQ comes from, and it is why orders have to land on multiples.

Do not type an MOQ. Your minimum is not a number you choose, it is a number your kettle chooses. Declare the batch honestly and the floor falls out, along with the lattice: the multiples an order must land on. Type a round number instead and you will either quote jobs you lose money on, or turn away jobs you could have taken.

Commercial defaults

Set once here, inherited by every product you build. Override any of them on an individual product when that product genuinely differs. This is the retyping you never have to do again.

Every line below is partner-set and creator-paid, so it sits inside the production subtotal and carries the platform fee like your unit price does. It is not a side charge, it is not hidden from the creator, and none of it is shaved off your payout. Anything you leave at zero simply is not charged.
