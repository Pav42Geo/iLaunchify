Platform Vision · Feature Strategy & Design Brief

Let creators invent their own product — and build it with a manufacturer, without ever leaving the platform.

A new module that turns your marketplace from “order a template” into “launch your own brand.” Creators post an idea or a formula, matched manufacturers raise their hand, and the whole build — recipe, label, packaging, approvals — happens inside one guided, on-platform workspace.

01 · The big bet

What we’re actually building

Today creators can order and tweak ready-made templates. This feature lets them originate — bring their own recipe, or their own idea, and get a real branded CPG product made under their control. Almost no one serves the small creator here. That’s the whitespace.

The gap

Nobody does this for small creators

Keychain & Pietra serve people who already know what to make and just need a factory. The creator who has an audience and an idea but no formulation knowledge is unserved.

The moat

The build stays on-platform

Recipe, label and packaging move as structured, approvable objects — not email attachments. That’s the reason to stay, and it’s very hard to copy.

The risk to manage

Trust in a first-of-kind service

We’re first, so we must be the most responsible: IP protection, staged reveal, milestone escrow and clear creator control from message one.

The one-sentence strategy: Be the place where a creator’s idea becomes a shippable product through a guided, trustworthy, on-platform collaboration — so leaving for email feels like a downgrade, not an escape.

02 · Market landscape

Who’s near this space — and the door they left open

The manufacturing-marketplace category is real and funded. But it’s built for brands and retailers who already have a product spec. The creator co-creation layer — especially for the non-expert — is missing.

Keychain

Brands / retailers

AI matchmaker across 30k+ manufacturers. Type a product, see who can make it, reserve line time. Cuts sourcing from 12–18 months to days. But: you must know your product; built for established brands, not idea-stage creators.

Pietra

Creators / DTC

Closest analog: 1,500+ vetted factories, sourcing → fulfillment → storefront, launch in 60–90 days. But: largely white-label/private-label & catalog sourcing. Light on guided formulation for a novel recipe and on structured, in-app recipe/label approval.

Wonnda

EU · B2B

Private-label & co-manufacturing marketplace connecting brands with FMCG makers. Discovery-first. But: B2B sourcing tool, not a creator-first co-creation & collaboration experience.

COLLAB / agencies

Top talent only

Full-service “we build your creator brand” — great outcomes, but hands-on, high-touch, and reserved for celebrities/large creators. But: doesn’t scale to the long tail of small creators. That tail is our market.

Anti-pattern to avoid — 99designs-style open bidding. Widely criticized as exploitative: makers do unpaid spec work, race to “safe” output, and get one shot. Manufacturers will never do free formulation work on spec. The market has moved to curated matching (Toptal-style). We take the good part — competition — without the toxic part — free labor.
03 · Who we’re serving

Two creators, two on-ramps

Same destination — their own product on a shelf. Different starting knowledge. The UI must greet each correctly on the very first screen.

Type 1 · “The Formulator”

Knows the recipe / formula

Chefs, food nomads, cosmetic & wellness enthusiasts, product nerds. They have a formula (or the key ingredients) and want it made their way.

  • Wants: fidelity to their spec, minimal dilution of vision
  • Fear: “the factory will change my recipe / steal it”
  • On-ramp: “I have a recipe” → structured formula builder
Type 2 · “The Visionary”

Has the idea, not the know-how

The passion-fruit protein water creator. Strong affinity & audience, zero formulation skill. Needs a partner to make the idea real — under their control.

  • Wants: a guide/expert, confidence, staying in charge
  • Fear: “I’ll get railroaded or ripped off”
  • On-ramp: “I have an idea” → guided concept brief

Design implication: one entry screen, two doors — “I have a recipe” and “I have an idea.” Both funnel into the same Product Brief object, just with different amounts pre-filled. Everything downstream is shared machinery.

04 · The matching model

Not an auction. A curated “raise your hand,” then a paid discovery.

The hardest strategic choice is how creators and manufacturers find each other. Here’s the recommendation and why.

ModelHow it worksVerdict
Open bidding / contest
99designs-style		 Manufacturers submit real formulations to win the job. Reject Free spec work makers won’t do; races to cheap; erodes trust.
Pure algorithmic match
Keychain-style		 AI assigns the “best” factory instantly. Partial Great for known specs; too cold for an emotional, first-time creator build.
Curated RFQ + Expression of Interest
recommended		 Creator posts a brief → platform routes to fit-matched manufacturers → makers send a lightweight Expression of Interest (capabilities, ballpark range, MOQ, why-us, timeline — no free recipe) → creator shortlists & picks one → a paid Discovery & Sample phase begins. Adopt Competition without exploitation; creator stays in control; makers invest only once chosen.

Cheap to express interest

Interest = structured card (fit, range, MOQ, lead time, credentials, one note). Minutes, not unpaid labor. Keeps the pool liquid.

Expensive to actually build

The real work — formulation, samples — is a paid milestone after selection. Makers only invest in deals they’ve won.

Creator always decides

Platform matches & ranks; the creator shortlists and chooses. Never auto-assigned. Control is the product.

Trend read (2026): across marketplaces the momentum is RFQ + curation + verified profiles + escrow/milestones, away from race-to-the-bottom auctions. We match that, and add the CPG-specific collaboration layer nobody else has.

07 · The collaboration workspace

Why they never want to leave

Messenger-style chat is table stakes and, alone, a trap — it’s where “email me the recipe” happens. The winning model organizes the relationship around structured objects with states, with chat as connective tissue.

🧩 Objects, not attachments

Recipe, label, packaging, sample, spec sheet, quote, PO — each is a first-class object with a lifecycle: Draft → Submitted → In Review → Changes Requested → Approved → Locked. Reuse the exact recipe/label forms from your Partner “Add Product” flow, so submission feels native to makers.

🕓 Versioning & side-by-side

Every re-submission is a version. Creators compare v1⇄v2, see exactly what changed (like the base % in the wireframe). A decision log records who approved what, when — the audit trail that makes trust real.

📌 Contextual feedback

Comments attach to a line of the formula or a pin on the label — not lost in a chat river. Threaded replies, @mentions, resolve/re-open. Feedback is actionable, tied to the thing it’s about.

🚦 Guided stage gates

The room shows the current phase and the one action that unblocks it. Novice creators are never lost; makers always know what’s expected next. Nudges + status keep momentum without a project manager.

Design principle: if a task can be done in email, it must be easier in the room. Submitting a recipe as a form, approving with one tap, pinning a label note — each must beat the email alternative on effort and on safety.
08 · Trust, IP & payments

Because we’re first, we have to be the most responsible

This is the part that makes a creator brave enough to hand over their idea. Get it visibly right and it becomes the reason they choose us.

🔒 Staged reveal

  • Public brief shows intent, not the secret formula
  • Full recipe/targets only revealed after selection + mutual NDA, inside the private room
  • Need-to-know: the maker can’t export the raw formula without a logged, watermarked action

📜 NDA ≠ ownership

  • Auto mutual NDA on entry (confidentiality)
  • Plus explicit IP terms: creator owns the recipe, brand & label; maker’s background IP stays theirs
  • Improvements & “who owns what” defined up front, in-platform, in plain language

💰 Milestone escrow

  • Discovery, samples, tooling, production run = separate milestones
  • Funds held in escrow, released on creator approval of each object
  • Dispute & refund policy with the decision log as evidence

✅ Verification & reputation

Manufacturers are vetted (certifications, allergens, capacity, capabilities) before entering the pool. Two-sided reviews and completed-build counts build accountability. Fit-score + track record, not just price, drive matching.

🧭 Creator control, always

Nothing advances without the creator’s approval. They can pause, request changes, or (within policy) switch makers before production. “Under their control” is encoded into every gate — the fear from Persona 2 answered structurally.

09 · How it makes money

Aligned with successful launches, not gated at the door

A platform fee on each paid milestone / production run — the core, usage-aligned revenue.

Sub

Maker seats

Manufacturers pay for pool access, more concurrent rooms, and better placement/analytics.

+

Value-add services

Design, compliance/label review, samples, photography, fulfillment — attach margin along the rail.

Esc

Escrow / payments

Float and payment spread on escrowed milestones as volume scales.

Principle: keep posting a brief free (fills the pool), monetize when real money and real work move. Don’t tax the idea — tax the outcome.

10 · How to ship it

Crawl → walk → run

1

Concierge MVP weeks, not quarters

Ship the Brief builder, a curated pool for a hand-picked set of verified makers in ONE category (e.g., functional beverages), and a basic room with the recipe/label/pack objects + approvals. Your team manually matches at first. Prove the loop end-to-end with 5–10 real creator builds.

2

Self-serve matching + escrow

Automate fit-matching & the live opportunity feed, add milestone escrow, versioning & the decision log, two-sided reviews. Open a second and third product category. Introduce maker subscriptions.

3

Scale the rail

Add AI brief-assist for novices, label/compliance checks, sample logistics, self-design on maker dielines, and fulfillment/storefront hand-off. This is where you leapfrog Pietra/Keychain on the creator co-creation experience.

Biggest risks & the counters:
  • Cold-start liquidity → seed makers first in one category; concierge-match so early briefs always get quality interest.
  • Disintermediation (“let’s just email”) → make in-room the easier and safer path; tie escrow, IP protection & dispute support to staying on-platform.
  • Trust in a novel service → over-invest in verification, staged reveal, and visible creator control from day one.
  • Quality variance → structured objects + approvals + reviews make bad outcomes visible early and rare.
